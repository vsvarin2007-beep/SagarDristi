// PROBLEM STATEMENT
The Challenge of Maritime Oil Spill Detection
Oil spills in maritime regions pose serious environmental and economic challenges. Detecting spills early and identifying their possible sources is difficult because of changing ocean conditions and incomplete vessel tracking information.
01 // DETECTION COMPLEXITY
Difficult Oil Spill Detection
• Oil spills can be difficult to identify in satellite imagery.
• Natural phenomena such as low-wind areas and biogenic films can resemble oil slicks.
• Traditional image thresholding may produce false detections.
02 // TRACKING CONSTRAINTS
Limited Vessel Tracking
• AIS provides information about vessel positions, speed, and direction.
• AIS data may be unavailable, incomplete, or inconsistent.
• Vessel tracking alone cannot confirm whether a ship caused an oil spill.
03 // OCEAN DYNAMICS
Changing Ocean Conditions
• Wind, currents, and waves influence oil spill movement.
• A spill may move far from its original location.
• Simple backward tracking may produce inaccurate source estimates.
04 // ATTRIBUTION AMBIGUITY
Difficult Source Attribution
• Multiple vessels may pass near a detected spill.
• Identifying a potential source requires combining spatial, temporal, and movement information.
• Comprehensive multi-variable cross-referencing is necessary for investigation.
🎯 Project Objective: Develop an intelligent system that detects oil spill candidates from satellite imagery and correlates their movement with maritime vessel data.

// AI-BASED DETECTION
Deep Learning-Based Satellite Image Segmentation
SagarDrishti uses deep learning-based segmentation to identify potential oil spill regions in Sentinel-1 SAR imagery.
STEP 01
Image Preprocessing
• Prepare satellite imagery for model input.
• Apply normalization and image filtering.
• Utilize available SAR polarizations (VV/VH) and derived radiometric features.
• Mask non-marine areas using coastal boundary data.
STEP 02 (MODEL)
U-Net++ Segmentation
• Encoder-decoder architecture with nested dense skip pathways.
• Classifies image pixels to separate candidate spill regions from the sea surface.
• Effectively resolves low-contrast slick boundaries.
• Generates clean pixel-level probability masks.
STEP 03
Spill Region Extraction
• Identify connected components in segmentation outputs.
• Delineate slick boundaries and morphological contours.
• Calculate exact geographic coordinates (lat/long).
• Estimate approximate affected spill surface area.

// DRIFT ANALYSIS
Understanding Spill Movement Through Ocean Dynamics
Oil spills move under the influence of environmental conditions. SagarDrishti uses drift analysis to estimate how a detected spill may have moved and to investigate possible source locations.
INFLUENCING FACTORS
Key Environmental Factors
• Ocean Currents: Primary advective forcing driving the bulk displacement of surface slicks.

• Wind Direction & Speed: Surface windage directly influences slick drift velocity and elongation.

• Wave-Induced Movement: Stokes drift and wave action contribute to surface spreading and dispersion.

• Spill Observation Time: The elapsed interval between initial discharge and satellite capture.

• Geographic Constraints: Shorelines, islands, and shallow passages that restrict possible drift trajectories.
SYSTEM WORKFLOW
5-Step Drift Modeling Workflow
1. Spill Observation: Identify the detected spill location from satellite imagery.

2. Environmental Data Integration: Ingest available wind and ocean current data for surrounding conditions.

3. Forward Drift Modeling: Estimate possible spill dispersion under environmental forces.

4. Backward Drift Estimation: Trace possible movement paths backward from observed spill region.

5. Source Region Estimation: Identify a candidate region from which the spill may have originated.
⚠️ Key Point: Drift analysis estimates possible source regions; it does not independently prove the exact discharge location.
// VESSEL ATTRIBUTION
Identifying Potential Vessels Associated with an Oil Spill
SagarDrishti correlates estimated spill source regions with available AIS vessel movement data to identify vessels that may have been present during the incident.
CORRELATION CRITERIA
Vessel Correlation Factors
1. Spatial Proximity: Compare vessel positions with the estimated spill source region to identify vessels that passed near the candidate location.

2. Temporal Alignment: Compare vessel movement times with estimated spill observation and drift periods.

3. Vessel Movement: Analyze vessel speed and course changes to identify unusual movement patterns for investigation.

4. AIS Data Availability: Check availability and continuity of vessel tracking data, identifying gaps or inconsistencies.
PROCESS & OUTPUT
Attribution Workflow & Deliverables
Attribution Pipeline:
Satellite Spill Detection ➔ Source Region Estimation ➔ AIS Track Comparison ➔ Candidate Vessel Ranking

Expected Outputs:
• Potentially associated vessels list
• Vessel trajectory and track visualization
• Spatial and temporal correlation information
• Ranked candidate vessels for prioritized investigation

Results are presented in structured tables to assist maritime authorities.
⚖️ Important: A vessel appearing near a spill is not proof of responsibility. The system supports investigation and prioritization, not legal determination.

Spill Movement Prediction & Environmental Risk Analysis
SagarDrishti can use available environmental data to estimate possible future spill movement and support monitoring of affected coastal regions.

Forward Drift Prediction

• Incorporate available wind and ocean current forecast information.
• Estimate possible future spill trajectories over operational horizons.

COASTAL RISK
Coastal Risk Assessment
• Identify coastal regions near predicted spill trajectories.
• Highlight shoreline areas that may require further monitoring.
• Support the prioritization of environmentally sensitive locations (mangroves, coral reefs, ports).
APPLICATIONS
Potential Applications

ℹ️ Note: Prediction accuracy depends on the quality of environmental data and the drift model. Forecasts are estimates, not guaranteed spill paths.
